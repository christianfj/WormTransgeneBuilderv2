Everything the app uses lives in `inst/extdata/` (human-readable source tables) and `data/` (the same
information in R's binary format, built by the scripts in `dev/`). The **Source and licence** column must be
confirmed by the authors before the first release: the entries marked *to be completed* are not recorded
anywhere in the code, so they are not guessed here.

## Codon table columns

`Codon`, `Amino` (one letter; `X` = stop). Every column was checked on 2026-10-07 by recomputing it from public data with `dev/verify_codon_table.R` (needs internet; it downloads the coding sequences from WormBase ParaSite and Serizay et al.'s Supplemental Table S2). The results:

| Column | Meaning | Used for | Checked by recomputation |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Ubiq` | Relative frequency of each codon among the codons of its amino acid (sums to 1 per amino acid) in the **500 genes with the highest mean expression across the five tissues of Serizay et al. 2020, among the genes classified as ubiquitous** ("Ubiq.", 3,393 genes; expression = mean of the five tissue TPM columns of Table S2) | "Ubiquitous expression" method; weights for random choices in the shields | Reproduced exactly (differences below 1e-10) with the longest isoform of each gene in WormBase ParaSite WBPS19. |
| `Chance` | Equal weight (1 / number of synonymous codons) for every codon of an amino acid; the three stop codons get 1/3 each | "Random recoding" | Exact. |
| `CAI1` | 1 for the best codon of each amino acid and 0 for the others (all three stop codons are 1) | "High expression" method | Cannot be recomputed: it comes from the web tool (below). |
| `BringMansWeights` | Graded weights, 1 for the best codon of each amino acid and below 1 for the others (0 for the stop codons) | CAI of the "High expression" method; ranking changes in its shields; candidates for its ribosome-binding-site step | Cannot be recomputed: it comes from the web tool (below). |

*Columns removed on 2026-10-07* (the app did not use them; the last commit that has them is ab0fdcb): `Germ`, `Neur`, `Soma` (the same rule as `Ubiq` for the germline-specific, neuron-specific and soma-restricted classes; reproduced to a mean difference of 0.0001), `aCDS` (codon usage of all annotated coding sequences, every isoform; reproduced to within 0.0006) and `HighRCSU` (relative synonymous codon usage, mean 1 per amino acid; not reproducible exactly, closest to the 200 most highly expressed genes of the Serizay data). Before they were removed we noted that `HighRCSU` preferred GCT for alanine and GAT for aspartate while `CAI1` has GCC and GAC (each pair almost tied); the two amino acids should be compared with the web tool's output.

**`CAI1` and `BringMansWeights`.** They were derived from the *C. elegans* Codon Adapter, the web tool of Redemann S, Schloissnig S, Ernst S, Pozniakowsky A, Ayloo S, Hyman AA and Bringmann H (2011), Codon adaptation-based control of protein expression in *C. elegans*, Nat Methods 8:250-252, doi:10.1038/nmeth.1565 (https://worm.mpi-cbg.de/codons/cgi-bin/optimize.py; H. Bringmann is an author of the app's paper). From the paper:

* **Definition of the CAI.** "The codon adaptation index (CAI) of a gene is defined as the geometric mean of the codon weights of all codons of that gene. The weight of a codon is determined by a reference set of genes that are known to be highly expressed (Sharp & Li 1987). If a gene has a CAI value of 1.0, this gene consists only of codons optimal for fast translation." The app's `calc_cai()` is this geometric mean (codons with weight 0, the stop codons, are left out).
* **Weights.** "We used codon weights determined from a set of highly expressed genes", reference 12 of the paper: Carbone A, Zinovyev A, Kepes F, Bioinformatics 19:2005-2015 (2003). This is the source of the best codon per amino acid (`CAI1`) and of the graded weights (`BringMansWeights`). The set of genes is not listed in the paper.
* **The tool's algorithm.** The sequence is held as a series of codons; codons are replaced one by one until the requested CAI is reached or nothing can be changed further, with the optimized codons "spread as evenly as possible throughout the sequence". Options: resistance to RNAi of the endogenous gene (no stretch of 7 unaltered codons), a ribosome-binding-site step (nucleotides 1 to 37 are sampled at random from the 37-mers that encode the same amino acids and the one with weak RNA structure and favorable CAI is chosen, using the Vienna RNA package; positions -4 to -1 are always `aaaa`) and removal of splice sites (NetGene2). The app's own ribosome-binding-site step (`optimize_rbs()`, 39 bases, 100 random candidates folded with RNAfold, `aaaa` upstream) follows this idea but is a separate implementation; the other steps of the tool (target CAI below 1, RNAi resistance, splice-site removal) are not in the app.
* **Data.** The three artificial introns of the paper are the `Fire_lab_introns` of the app (see the table above); the Supplementary Table 1 of the paper (codon usage and CAI of genomes) was not available to us.

**Verified against the tool (2026-10-07).** The result page of the tool contains, in plain HTML and in the JavaScript arrays that draw its "Codon Weights Details" bar chart, the weight of every codon of the input, the original and the optimized codons. `data-raw/codon_adapter/query_codon_adapter.R` submits the form (method POST, fields `name`, `sequence`, `introns`, `score`, ...) with one sequence that contains every codon once and saves what comes back (`codon_adapter_weights.csv`); `compare_with_app.R` compares it with the codon table. Result: **all 61 sense codons have exactly the weights stored in `BringMansWeights`** (largest difference 0.0000 at the tool's three-decimal precision), the three stop codons have weight 0, **every amino acid has the same best codon in the tool (weight 1, and the codon its optimizer chooses at CAI 1.0) as in `CAI1`**, including alanine (GCC) and aspartate (GAC). A test pins this. In a browser the same numbers are visible with "View page source" after pressing Optimize.

## Rebuilding

* `Rscript dev/make_enzy.R` rebuilds `data/enzy.rda` from `Enzymes.csv` (and checks its format).
* `Rscript dev/make_promoters.R file.tsv` rebuilds `Promoters.csv` from a tab-separated list.
* `data/glo_median_score.rda` is the median of all GLO scores; it is used as the score of a 12-mer that contains an `N`.
* `data/codon_freqs.rda` (the six columns of the CSV) was built by `inst/extdata/codon_data.R`; `Rscript dev/verify_codon_table.R` recomputes the columns that can be recomputed.
* `data/glo_median_score.rda` was built by `inst/extdata/data-raw:convert_glo_db.R` from a source file that is not kept in the repository.

The automated tests (`tests/testthat/test-data_files.R`) check that the tables and the built data files agree.

## WormBase and the Alliance of Genome Resources

Several tables use WormBase data: the type 1 piRNAs (WormBase release WS298), the gene names and identifiers of the promoters, 3' UTRs and native introns, the gene models of the native introns, and the coding sequences used to check the codon table. WormBase is moving its infrastructure to the Alliance of Genome Resources, whose web site (https://www.alliancegenome.org, version 9.1.0) was used for the checks of October 2026.

* **Citation.** Sternberg PW, Van Auken K, Wang Q, Wright A, Yook K, Zarowiecki M, et al. (2024), WormBase 2024: status and transitioning to Alliance infrastructure, Genetics 227(1):iyae050, doi:10.1093/genetics/iyae050 (open access, CC BY 4.0).
* **Licence.** According to the Alliance's licensing page (https://www.alliancegenome.org/privacy-warranty-licensing, printed 2026-10-14), all annotations and data produced by Alliance members that are accessible from alliancegenome.org are distributed under a **CC BY 4.0** licence (so they may be reused with attribution); data provided by these resources that the Alliance does not distribute may have different terms. Alliance software is under the MIT licence. The data are provided "as is", without warranty. The app's use is therefore covered by citing the paper above and stating the WormBase release.
* **WormBase ParaSite.** The genome sequences of the other *Caenorhabditis* species (piRNA databases) and the coding sequences used to check the codon table come from WormBase ParaSite (release WBPS19, March 2024, which corresponds to WormBase WS291).
