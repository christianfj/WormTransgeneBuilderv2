# WormTransgeneBuilder 2.0.0 (2026-10-14)
* **Changed results: Germline optimization (GLO) now finds the best possible coding.** The earlier GLO optimizer tried one random synonymous codon at a time and reached only about half of the score of the published method (Fielmich et al. 2018), because the best 12-mers need four codons to change together. `optimize_glo()` is now a port of the published algorithm (four-codon words, a first assembly, then improvement), which reaches exactly the scores of the original code on the three proteins tested (35 and 238 residues, and a random protein of 150 residues; `docs/GLO_VERIFICATION.md`). By default it goes one step further and finds the **exact optimum** by dynamic programming over windows of four codons (`method = "exact"`); the port of the published algorithm is still available as `method = "heuristic"` and falls 0.1 to 2 % below the optimum. The 12-mer score table was not changed (it is identical to the original database).
* **Changed results: GLO tie-break and shields.** Many different codings share the best GLO score; among them the one with the highest Codon Adaptation Index (Ubiquitous table) is chosen, so GLO results are now deterministic (the random jitter is off by default). Restriction sites of the selected enzymes are avoided inside the optimization itself, and when a restriction site or a piRNA match still has to be repaired in a GLO sequence, the change that costs the least GLO score is chosen (it used to be a random, Ubiquitous-weighted choice).
* **Changed display: the GLO score is shown per codon** (the score divided by the number of codons, as the original method reports it), in the results and in the GenBank comment ("GLO Score per codon"), so that sequences of different length can be compared.
* **Changed results: intron spacing follows the median exon.** With "Early start", the first intron is now placed near codon 41 and the following ones every 41 codons (one median exon of 123 bp, Spieth et al. 2014), instead of codon 50 and every 50 codons (150 bp). The warning about short exons now appears only when the average exon would be shorter than 110 bp (it was 150 bp). "Equidistant" placement is unchanged.
* **Changed results: the Twist synthesis check follows Twist's guidelines of October 2026.** It now reports *standard guidelines* and *high complexity* separately. New: homopolymers longer than 30 bp, 50 bp windows below 10 % or above 90 % GC, direct repeats longer than 200 bp (high complexity); length limits (300 to 7,000 bp) and a ccdB check (standard). A 50 bp window above 80 % GC is now a warning. **Bug fixed:** the check ignored all lower-case bases, so introns, UTRs and promoters were never checked. Details in `docs/METHODS.md`.
* **Changed tag:** the "15 AA glycine-serine flexible linker" was by mistake a copy of the 12 amino acid linker. It is replaced by a **16 amino acid (GGGS)4 linker** (48 bases), the longer linker of Waldo et al. 1999. Anyone who used the old entry got the 12 amino acid linker.
* **Renamed:** the native intron set *rps-0* is now **rpsa-1** (the gene was renamed; WBGene00004469). The introns are unchanged (checked against the current gene model); only the name in the menu and in the log changes. The label "Lyssennko" in the tag table is corrected to Lyssenko.
* **Renamed:** the 3' UTR menu entry *rps-1* is now **rps-3A** (the gene was renamed; WBGene00004470). The sequence is unchanged; only the name in the menu, the viewer and the GenBank annotation changes. The sources of the three 3' UTRs are in `docs/DATA.md`.
* **Changed results: rebuilt piRNA database and a choice of species.** The *C. elegans* database was rebuilt from primary sources (WormBase WS298 type 1 piRNAs with their sequences cut from the genome, the 2,485 type 2 piRNAs of the pirScan set, and 359 further sequences enriched in PRG-1 immunoprecipitation; Claycomb lab and Billi et al. data for abundance and enrichment) and now has 18,207 entries (it had 17,849). Under "Minimize piRNA homology" the user now chooses whose piRNAs to avoid: *C. elegans* (default), and optionally *C. briggsae* (25,883 PRG-1 IP loci, high confidence) and *C. remanei* and *C. brenneri* (11,801 and 17,991 piRNAs called from small-RNA sequencing, low confidence); several species can be selected at once. The *C. elegans* type 2 piRNAs have their own tick box (on by default). The databases are independent and are only combined when selected. The results table names the species and the type of each match. See `docs/PIRNA_DATABASE.md` and `docs/PIRNA_OTHER_SPECIES.md`.
* Screening against several species takes longer: for a 3 kb gene the whole design takes about 1.4 s with the defaults and about 3 s with *C. elegans* and *C. briggsae* (4.5 s with all four species); for a 20 kb gene about 7 s, 18 s and 28 s (`dev/benchmarks/results/species_timing.csv`).
* The piRNA database no longer contains 301 entries that had no target sequence (17,849 piRNAs remain). They were not present in another list of piRNAs or on WormBase, so they are not validated, and with no sequence they could never be flagged or removed by the piRNA shield.
* "High expression" recoding is now deterministic, and its restriction-site and piRNA shields choose the change with the smallest loss of Codon Adaptation Index. Non-optimal codons are underlined in the sequence viewer.
* Restriction-site removal understands IUPAC ambiguity codes (for example BanI), the enzyme list is read from `Enzymes.csv`, and a site that cannot be removed now gives a warning. The random methods can fall back to a neighbouring codon.
* Synthetic introns are picked at random again for every method.
* The GGSGG linker tag no longer ends in a stop codon.
* piRNA screening is several times faster (about 6 times for a 3 kb gene and 11 times for a 20 kb gene), with identical results.
* Germline optimization (GLO) is faster and uses far less memory, with identical results: its 12-mer scores are found by arithmetic instead of searching a dictionary of names. The first GLO request after the app starts no longer takes about 9 seconds, GLO scoring is 18 to 34 times faster, the GLO optimization is 4 to 7 times faster, and the score data takes 128 MB of memory instead of 1.3 GB.
* Restriction-site removal is much faster (about 30 times for the "High expression" method with many enzymes, 15 times for the other methods), with identical results. Together with the piRNA and GLO speed-ups, a whole design of a 3 kb gene now takes about 1 second instead of 13 to 21 seconds.
* The restriction-enzyme list is now New England Biolabs' full catalogue of 234 enzymes (it had 21), including enzymes whose sites contain ambiguity letters or runs of any base (for example AhdI, SfiI, XcmI). DNA methylation (Dam/Dcm) is no longer recorded or used: a synthesized transgene is not methylated, so a site is treated as a site whatever the cloning host later does.
* The restriction-enzyme menu is grouped: "Commonly used" (22 enzymes), "Golden Gate" (8, including BsaI and BsmBI, which are listed only there) and "All other enzymes". There is deliberately no "select all", because removing many sites lowers how far a sequence can be optimized. No enzyme is pre-selected any more (BsaI used to be).
* The promoter list is expanded to 38 validated promoters (300 bp) in two groups, "Validated single neuron promoters" and "Validated tissue-specific promoters". The menu shows the name and the gene (for example "pADF (WBGene00005359, srh-142)"); the sequence viewer and the GenBank file use the name only ("pADF"). Group headings cannot be chosen. The earlier five promoters were replaced by this list (the old "DD" promoter, Y48G10A.6, is not in the new list).
* Reproducible build: R packages are pinned with `renv.lock`, and the new Docker image is built from R 4.5.3 on Ubuntu 24.04 (pinned by digest) with ViennaRNA 2.7.2 compiled from checksum-verified source. The About tab and the start-up message show the app, R and ViennaRNA versions. A fixed run gives identical output on the Mac and in the arm64 and amd64 images.
* **Changed result:** "High expression" with the ribosome-binding-site step now always recodes the start. Before, the folding step could pick the input codons for the first 13 codons (in 43% of 60 random test starts) because the method's own recoding gave only one candidate. The 100 candidates for the start are now drawn with the graded codon weights; the app uses a fixed seed for this combination, so a given input still gives a given output.
* **Changed default:** the minimum number of mismatches to known piRNAs (the slider under "Minimize piRNA homology") now starts at 4 instead of 3, because in our experiments with synthetic guide piRNAs four mismatches abolished silencing while up to three were tolerated (Priyadarshini et al. 2022). Windows that were safe at 3 but have exactly 3 mismatches are now repaired by default. The slider still goes from 0 to 4.
* **Changed results: germline optimization (GLO) now finds the best possible score, and works together with the shields.** The score is a sum over windows of four codons, so the best coding is found exactly by dynamic programming (0.1 to 2% above the original algorithm, which is kept as `method = "heuristic"`; 0.5 s for 20 kb); among equally scoring codings the one with the highest Codon Adaptation Index is chosen. Restriction sites to be removed are avoided inside the optimization, and the piRNA shield and any remaining sites are removed with the change that costs the least GLO score, instead of random changes (the shields used to cost about 5% of the GLO score on test genes, now 2 to 3%).
* (Earlier the same day) germline optimization (GLO) followed the original algorithm. The earlier search changed one codon at a time and reached only about half of the score of the published method; `optimize_glo()` is now a port of Dan Dickinson's original (four-codon words, first guess, context-aware refinement) and reaches exactly the scores the unchanged original code reaches on the test proteins (`docs/GLO_VERIFICATION.md`). The GLO step takes about 1 s for a 3 kb gene and 9 s for 20 kb.
* "Force in reading frame" for introns now works. Ticked (the default, and what the app always did before), an intron goes only between codons; unticked, it may go after any base, using the same AAG/CAG preference.
* The app no longer depends on Bioconductor (Biostrings) or stringr; it needs only CRAN packages and the external program ViennaRNA. One small difference: the optional piRNA highlighting in the sequence viewer no longer marks matches that would hang off the end of a sequence.
* Added an automated test suite and benchmark scripts.
